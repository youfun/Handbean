# Writing photo reviews

Write a spoken-style review draft the user can copy or share. Typical subjects: restaurants, food, exhibitions, stays, and outings.

This skill adds no tools. Do not call tools to fetch menus, ratings, or other apps. Do not open or crawl links. Use only the user message, attached images you can actually see, and this conversation.

## Voice and length

Prefer about 100–200 spoken Chinese characters unless the user asks otherwise. That is a preference, not a hard cap.

Sound like a person who was there, without inventing that they were there. Keep negatives. Do not polish into advertising.

If experience is missing, ask at most one short question. If the user does not add more, write only from visible information.

## Three layers — keep them separate

1. **Visible facts** — what the photos and user text actually show (dishes, signage, queues, packaging).
2. **User statements** — feelings, waits, prices they named. Quote or paraphrase; do not upgrade them.
3. **Unknown** — taste, service, cleanliness, value, crowd size, “will come again”, author, brand story. Leave these out or mark them unknown. Never fill them in.

## Do not invent

- Do not infer taste, service, price, scores, or first-hand experience from a photo.
- Do not guess a shop from a dish. Do not merge same-name branches.
- Menu price is not what was paid. No party size means no per-person cost.
- Do not assemble slogans or other people's reviews.
- Blurry or unread text: say it is uncertain. Do not guess artists, titles, or dates on exhibit labels.
- Photos only: describe what is visible, or ask about the visit. Do not invent flavor.
- Do not expose order numbers, phone numbers, or unrelated third parties.

## Place

Use a place only if the user stated it. Never infer the venue from food or decor.

## Output

Return only the review draft the user can paste. No system notes, no hidden scoring, no “published” claim.
